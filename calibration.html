Calibration · our error rate

How often we're wrong.

Every number we publish comes from a scorer, and every scorer makes mistakes. This page is where we publish ours. If you can't find a verifier's error rate, you can't use their number, so this page exists before our first audit does.

First calibration study in progress

We are double-scoring public piece-picking footage, the automatic scorer and a human independently on the same picks, to produce the first agreement figures. They will appear here in the form shown below the moment they exist, and we will not issue a report before they do.

Until then, no agreement figure appears anywhere on this site. A specimen report shows the format with illustrative values and is labelled as such.

What will appear here

Overall agreement. Cohen's κ between the automatic scorer and the human scorer, across all double-scored picks, with a 95% interval and the number of picks it rests on.

Confusion matrix. For each of the six rubric outcomes, how often the scorer and the human agreed and, when they disagreed, what each said. This is the figure that matters most: a scorer that confuses "drop" with "success" is dangerous; one that confuses "miss" with "no attempt" is not.

By item class. Agreement broken out by rigid, bagged, deformable, and transparent or reflective items, because that is where scorers fail.

By scorer version. Every prompt or model version we have used, dated, with its own figures. Versions are never edited in place.

How error enters a report

The scorer's measured misclassification rates are used to correct the point estimate and are carried into the interval. A weaker scorer makes the interval wider; it does not move the point in the scorer's favour. When agreement on a given item class falls below the threshold set in the protocol, that class is reported as Insufficient data rather than as a number.

Human scorers

Human scorers see the rubric and the pick, and never the automatic result. They are paid by the pick regardless of the outcome they record. Their own inter-rater agreement is measured on a shared subset and published alongside the scorer's.
