The refusal record

Four things we tested and will not sell you

A measurement company is worth exactly what its refusals are worth. Each of these was pre-registered, scored in a frozen order, and killed by its own criterion.

Seed selection Picking the best of eight seeds on the suite average did lift the held-out suite number — and silently damaged an average of 1.8 of 10 tasks by ≥5 points (worst −10.8) in 97.5% of draws. The pre-registered rule refused suite-average selection as a product rule, whatever the gain. We do not sell it. refused
Forecasting your σ We built a model to predict a cell's noise floor from its atlas row, then tested it on four held-out cells across two model families. One calibrated. Every miss overestimated the tail. The line is closed — we cannot forecast your noise floor and will not imply otherwise. closed
Agent evaluation We ran two pre-registered studies on public coding-agent benchmarks. The composite came back indeterminate, then the frontier models split against each other. The line is paused. No part of this engine has been validated on agent evaluations, and we do not sell it for them. paused
Priors on your stack The gate refuses to price an evaluation with a σ measured in a different success-rate regime — it returns INVALID with the reason attached rather than transferring a number that does not apply. Rows declaring a success-rate band are enforced, not advisory. enforced

And one we got wrong, on the record

A pre-registered prediction said at least one of eight new seeds would land below 60.0 on a collapsing task. The minimum was 60.5 — falsified by half a point, inside the corpus resolvability floor. The prediction stayed on the record and its stated consequence was refused.

Every result behind this page was pre-registered: decision bands frozen and hashed before a single episode was scored. When a pre-check failed, the pre-committed demotion applied and the weaker claim shipped — which is why two rows in the atlas are marked unpaired rather than quietly reported as paired.

Method

How a cell gets measured

J independent retrains on byte-identical data — same dataset hash, same budget, same config, training seed varied — then evaluated at a fixed eval seed. On PushT and ALOHA the seed fixes the initial-state list; on LIBERO it does not — that harness walks its own init-state pointer, so LIBERO rows are common-seed-list, not common-episode-set. σ_run is unaffected either way: it needs the episode set per task to be comparable, not episode-by-episode identity. σ is estimated from the retrain gaps with a χ² interval, and the binomial component at the stated n is subtracted to separate eval sampling from the training lottery. Both readings are carried.

Measuring your own cell

The protocol above is the whole method and it is free to run — you need J retrains on data you already have and an eval you already run. Nothing about it requires us, and nothing about it requires sending anyone your checkpoints, your data, or your GPU time.

What we do is the part you cannot do for yourself: read the results back independently, apply the same frozen criteria we applied to our own waves, and issue a signed record of what your numbers do and do not support — including when the answer is that they support nothing yet.

Every value on this page was measured on the ORBIT research programme between June and August 2026 under pre-registration, and is reproduced from the shipped orbit_eval.atlas module rather than retyped. Success rates are percentage points throughout.

Scope: all sixteen cells were measured in simulation, on PushT, LIBERO and ALOHA sim with ACT, DiffusionPolicy, SmolVLA and π₀.₅ policies. No cell was measured on physical hardware. Transfer to another environment, policy or harness is a pre-registered hypothesis, not a result.
