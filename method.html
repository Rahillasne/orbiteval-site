Method · version 1 · piece picking

The procedure, in full.

Fixed before we look at a single pick. Every number on a report can be re-derived from it.

In one minute

  • ScopePiece picking. The in-scope SKU list is declared in writing before the window opens.
  • Pre-registerRubric, sampling rule, sample size, threshold: written, hashed, timestamped, then released with the report.
  • SampleWe choose the site and the window. Picks are drawn by a published seed, stratified by item class.
  • ScoreA model scores every pick. A human re-scores at least a fifth, blind. Disagreement widens the interval.
  • StatisticsWilson interval at 95%, adjusted for clustering by SKU and for scorer error. The claim is judged against it.
  • VerdictOne status per grade page: Current, Insufficient data, or Revoked. Re-checked monthly. Nothing deleted.

The detail

ScopeSingle items, source container to target container. Scope changes are logged and shown.

Version 1 covers robotic piece picking: single items moved from a source container to a target container. The in-scope SKU list is declared by the vendor and operator together, in writing, before the audit window opens. Changes to scope during the window are logged with a date and shown on the report. A pick on an item added to scope after the window opened is excluded from the sample and counted on a separate line.

Pre-registrationThe protocol is hashed (SHA-256) and the hash and timestamp are printed on the grade page.

Before any data is pulled we write a protocol document containing: the rubric version, the sampling rule and seed, the sample size and how it was chosen, the stratification, the calibration fraction, the interval method, and the threshold the claim will be tested against. The document is hashed and the hash and timestamp are printed on the report and the grade page. The document itself is released with the report.

SamplingSite drawn at random from the vendor's fleet; window is the four most recent complete weeks; 600–1,200 picks.

The population is every logged pick attempt on in-scope SKUs during the window. We choose the site and the window: the site is drawn at random from those the vendor has operated for at least eight weeks; the window is the four most recent complete weeks at pre-registration.

Picks are drawn by a seeded pseudo-random sampler, stratified by item class so rare classes are represented. The seed is published. Anyone with the vendor's log can regenerate exactly the picks we scored.

Sample size is set from the decision: the operator or vendor states the smallest gap worth detecting and the precision required (for example ±2 points at 95%), and we size the sample to deliver it after adjusting for clustering by SKU. A typical audit scores 600–1,200 picks.

RubricSix outcomes, exactly one per pick. The denominator is every presented, in-scope item.
OutcomeDefinition
SuccessOne correct item, placed in the target container, undamaged, without a human touching the item or the robot.
MissAttempt made, no item grasped.
DropItem grasped, then released before placement.
Double-pickMore than one item moved.
DamageItem or packaging visibly damaged by the pick, regardless of placement.
No attemptItem presented and in scope; the robot did not attempt it within the cycle.

The success rate is Success ÷ (all six outcomes). A vendor dashboard often excludes "no attempt"; where it does, the report shows both figures and says which one the claim referred to.

Scoring and calibrationEvery pick scored by a versioned model; at least 20% re-scored by a human who never sees the model's answer.

Every sampled pick is scored by an automatic scorer: a vision-language model given the rubric and the pick's video and log record, with a fixed, versioned prompt. A stratified fraction of at least 20% is also scored by a human who does not see the automatic result.

We report the agreement between the two (Cohen's κ) and the full confusion matrix by outcome. The scorer's measured misclassification rates correct the estimate and are carried into the interval, so a weaker scorer produces a wider interval rather than a wrong point. Prompt versions are numbered and never edited in place. Agreement figures across all audits are on the calibration page.

StatisticsPicks of one SKU are not independent. Cluster-adjusted Wilson interval, widened for scorer error.

Unit of inference. A robot that struggles with one transparent bag struggles with all of them, so picks are not independent and a naive interval is too narrow. We use a design effect for clustering by SKU, estimated from the sample itself.

Interval. Wilson score interval at 95%, cluster-adjusted, then widened for scorer misclassification. Outside the interval, the claim is not supported; inside it, the claim is consistent with the data at this sample size.

Breakdowns by item class and failure mode are descriptive unless pre-registered as tests. We say which. Trend is only called when a pre-registered test across re-checks rejects "no change".

The interval and sizing code is open: github.com/Rahillasne/orbit-eval.

VerdictsExactly one status per grade page.

Current  The latest re-check is within its validity window and the protocol was followed in full.

Insufficient data  The sample could not deliver the pre-registered precision. The report says what would resolve it. We do not round this up to a verdict.

Revoked  A re-check found the rate below the threshold; or logs were withheld or altered; or scope changed without being logged. The reason is on the grade page.

Re-checkingMonthly, same protocol, fresh seed. Every re-check stays on the page.

Every current grade is re-checked monthly with the same protocol and a fresh published seed. The grade page shows every re-check in sequence. Nothing is removed.

What we publishProtocol and hash, seed, sample size, agreement, interval, breakdowns, status, and an index of every scored pick.

We do not publish pick video without the operator's written permission; the index lets the operator or vendor find each pick in their own records. We never hold model weights or training data; the method does not need them.

IndependenceThe conflict policy is part of this method and versioned with it.

Method version 1, September 2026. Changes are versioned; a report always names the version it was produced under.
