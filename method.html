Method · version 1 · piece picking

The procedure, in full.

Everything below is fixed before we look at a single pick, and every number on a report can be re-derived from it. If you can't reproduce our sample and our arithmetic, you shouldn't trust our number.

Scope

Version 1 covers robotic piece picking: single items moved from a source container to a target container. The in-scope SKU list is declared by the vendor and operator together, in writing, before the audit window opens. Changes to scope during the window are logged with a date and shown on the report. A pick on an item that was added to scope after the window opened is excluded from the sample and counted in a separate line.

Pre-registration

Before any data is pulled we write a protocol document containing: the rubric version, the sampling rule and seed, the sample size and how it was chosen, the stratification, the calibration fraction, the interval method, and the threshold that the claim will be tested against. The document is hashed (SHA-256) and the hash and timestamp are printed on the report and the grade page. The document itself is released with the report.

Sampling

The population is every logged pick attempt on in-scope SKUs during the window. We choose the site and the window, from the vendor's active fleet, by a rule published on this page: the site is drawn at random from those the vendor has operated for at least eight weeks; the window is the four most recent complete weeks at the time of pre-registration.

Picks are drawn by a seeded pseudo-random sampler, stratified by item class so that rare classes are represented. The seed is published. Anyone with the vendor's log can regenerate exactly the picks we scored.

Sample size is set from the decision, not from convention: the operator or vendor states the smallest gap worth detecting and the precision required (for example, ±2 points at 95%), and we size the sample to deliver it after adjusting for clustering by SKU. A typical version 1 audit scores 600–1,200 picks.

Rubric

Each sampled pick is assigned exactly one outcome.

OutcomeDefinition
SuccessOne correct item, placed in the target container, undamaged, without a human touching the item or the robot.
MissAttempt made, no item grasped.
DropItem grasped, then released before placement.
Double-pickMore than one item moved.
DamageItem or packaging visibly damaged by the pick, regardless of placement.
No attemptItem presented and in scope; robot did not attempt it within the cycle.

The denominator is all six outcomes together: every presented, in-scope item. The success rate is Success ÷ (all outcomes). A vendor's dashboard often uses attempted picks as the denominator and excludes "no attempt"; where it does, the report shows both figures side by side and says which one the claim referred to.

Scoring and calibration

Every sampled pick is scored twice: once by an automatic scorer, a vision-language model given the rubric and the pick's video and log record with a fixed, versioned prompt, and, for a stratified fraction of at least 20% of the sample, once by a human scorer who does not see the automatic result.

We report the agreement between the two (Cohen's κ) and the full confusion matrix by outcome. The scorer's measured misclassification rates are used to correct the estimate and are carried into the interval, so a less reliable scorer produces a wider interval rather than a wrong point. Scorer prompt versions are numbered and never edited in place.

Our agreement figures across all audits are published on the calibration page and updated as they change.

Statistics

Unit of inference. Picks of the same SKU are not independent: a robot that struggles with one transparent bag struggles with all of them. Treating picks as independent gives an interval that is too narrow. We compute the interval on the success rate with a design effect for clustering by SKU, estimated from the sample itself.

Interval. Wilson score interval at 95%, cluster-adjusted, then widened for scorer misclassification. The claim is judged against the interval: outside it, the claim is not supported; inside it, the claim is consistent with the data at this sample size.

Breakdowns by item class and failure mode are descriptive unless they were pre-registered as tests. We say which.

Trend. A drift between re-checks is only called when a pre-registered test on the sequence of re-checks rejects "no change". A single month that looks worse is reported as what it is: one month.

The interval and sizing code is open and installable: github.com/Rahillasne/orbit-eval.

Verdicts

A grade page carries exactly one status.

Current  The most recent re-check is within its validity window and the pre-registered protocol was followed in full.

Insufficient data  The sample could not deliver the pre-registered precision, usually because too few in-scope picks were logged in the window. The report says what was measured and what sample would resolve it. We do not round this up to a verdict.

Revoked  A re-check found the measured rate below the published threshold; or the vendor withheld or altered logs; or scope changed without being logged. The reason is stated on the grade page.

Re-checking

Every current grade is re-checked monthly with the same protocol and a fresh published seed. The grade page shows every re-check in sequence. Nothing is removed.

What we publish

The protocol document and its hash, the seed, the sample size, the agreement figures, the interval, the breakdowns, the status, and an index of every scored pick by log identifier and timestamp.

We do not publish pick video without the operator's written permission; the index lets the operator or vendor find each pick in their own records. We never hold model weights or training data; the method does not need them.

Independence

The conflict policy on the front page is part of this method and is versioned with it.

Method version 1, September 2026. Changes are versioned; a report always names the version it was produced under.
