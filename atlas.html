A ranking of eight identical models

Here is a leaderboard. Eight entries, ranked by success rate on a standard manipulation benchmark, evaluated identically — 500 episodes each, the same eval seed, the same simulator build. The spread from first to last is 18.8 percentage points, which in this field is the difference between a paper and a rejection.

ALOHA sim_transfer_cube · success rate n=500 · eval seed 1000 · sync envs
They are the same model. One architecture, one dataset — the full 50-episode aloha_sim_transfer_cube_human set, byte-identical across all eight — one training recipe, 100k steps, pure LeRobot defaults. The only thing that differs is the training seed, 0 through 7.

The ranking is real. It is also entirely noise. Rank 1 beats rank 8 by 18.8 pp and there is nothing whatsoever to learn from it.

A leaderboard is a ranking of point estimates. Point estimates on these suites are draws from a distribution with a standard deviation we have now measured at 0.78 to 11.43 pp depending on the cell — and only 8% of the variance above came from evaluation sampling. The other 92% is the training run itself.

So we built the other thing. Not a ranking — a table of what a result has to beat before it means anything. Every row below is a measured noise floor for one cell of the space: a policy class, an environment, a data scale. It ranks nobody. It cannot be topped. It is simply the number you need before your number is interpretable.

What a success rate has to beat

σrun is the run-to-run standard deviation of a retrained checkpoint on a fixed episode set: same data, same budget, new training seed. It is the price of the question “is method A better than method B?” — and no amount of evaluation cleverness reduces it, because it lives in training, not in the eval draw.

Read this chart carefully — it is not an architecture ranking. The two ALOHA rows share an architecture, a protocol, an eval budget and k=50, and their σ in pp differs by 2.9×. In relative terms the ordering reverses: 8.4% of mean success for transfer-cube against 18.9% for insertion. The pp gap is where the success rate sits, not how stable the class is. That is why every low-SR row here carries a scope stamp, and why the gate refuses to price one of these cells with the other's σ.

The average is the wrong number

Multi-task suites report an average over tasks. Averaging shrinks noise by roughly √(number of tasks) — so the suite number looks reassuringly stable while each individual task keeps its full swing. And you do not deploy an average. You deploy the tasks.

We measured both quantities in the same waves, on the same retrains, in two policy classes. Per-task retrain σ is 2.6× to 3.2× the suite σ measured beside it.

The limits are the product

An atlas that overclaims is worse than no atlas, because it licenses confident decisions on unmeasured ground. Every row above is scoped, and the scope is machine-enforced: the gate refuses to price an evaluation with a σ measured in a different success-rate regime rather than silently transferring it.

Transfer across cells Every σ here was measured on PushT, LIBERO and ALOHA with DiffusionPolicy, ACT, SmolVLA and π₀.₅ policies. Transfer to other environments, policies or harnesses is a pre-registered hypothesis, not a result. Measure your own before trusting these. hypothesis
Suite-level wedge at k=88 At production data scale the suite-average excess over the eval floor was 0.61 pp against a 2 pp resolvability floor — indeterminate. The licensed finding is per-task, and only per-task. indeterminate
Low success-rate rows In pusht-act (43% of variance), aloha-insertion-act (41%) the binomial floor dominates. These σ values are substantially sampling, not retrain lottery, and must never be read as “this class is stable”. low-SR
ALOHA pairing The obs-hash alignment pre-check scored 0/6 on gym-aloha — EGL pixel render nondeterminism across processes, not a broken state mapping: a separate state-level probe found the seed→initial-qpos mapping byte-identical across fresh processes on both tasks, 10/10 seeds. The frozen demotion applied regardless, so both ALOHA rows are reported as unpaired common-seed-list measurements and no paired statistic is licensed against them. Exploratory split-half reads on these cells exist in the provenance record; they carry no pre-registration and are not quoted as results. unpaired
Cross-stack comparison A gym_pusht rebuild shifted absolute success rate by −18.2 pp, measured, and non-uniformly (range −10.6 to −24.4). Never pool or compare success rates across a simulator rebuild. invalid
The h=16 eval horizon The n_action_steps=16 row's success level came from an argmax sweep on one checkpoint and is optimistically biased — never quote it as ACT's PushT score. The σ estimand is far less affected: the non-argmax h=8 level gives 4.849 against 4.839. selection
A falsified prediction, kept PI05-CHAR's H1 predicted at least one of eight new seeds below 60.0 on the collapsing mask. The minimum was 60.5 — falsified by 0.5 pp, inside the corpus resolvability floor. The prediction stays on the record and its stated consequence was refused. on record

How it was measured

Each cell is J independent retrains on byte-identical data — same dataset hash, same budget, same configuration, training seed varied — then evaluated at a fixed eval seed. On PushT and ALOHA the seed fixes the initial-state list; on LIBERO it does not — that harness walks its own init-state pointer, so LIBERO rows are common-seed-list, not common-episode-set. σ_run is unaffected either way: it needs the episode set per task to be comparable, not episode-by-episode identity. σ is estimated from the retrain gaps with a χ² confidence interval, and the binomial component at the stated n is subtracted to separate eval sampling from the training lottery. Both readings are carried.

Decision bands were frozen and hashed before any episode was scored, and the frozen order of scoring is never changed after the fact. When a pre-check fails, the pre-committed demotion applies — which is why two rows here are marked unpaired rather than quietly reported as paired.

Rows declaring sr_range are enforced, not advisory: the gate refuses to price an evaluation whose binomial floor differs from every point of the band by more than 2×, in either direction.
