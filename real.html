Four answers, and never just better or worse.

Supported improvement

The change helped.

The whole interval sits above zero. The gap is bigger than what sampling alone produces at this number of attempts.

Supported regression

The change hurt.

The whole interval sits below zero. This is the only state that should stop a rollout on its own.

No material difference

Nothing worth acting on.

The interval covers zero and is tight enough to rule out a change as large as the one you said you would act on.

Insufficient evidence

You cannot tell yet.

The interval covers zero but is too wide to rule out a difference you would care about. The honest answer is more data, and the tool says how much.

What this does, and why it is not the usual arithmetic.

The interval

A Newcombe hybrid-score interval for the difference between two rates, not the textbook one. The textbook interval drifts below its promised coverage exactly where picking robots live, at success rates above 90%. We simulated both: at 95% against 95% with a hundred attempts a side, the textbook interval covers about 94.6% of the time and this one about 96.4%. Erring wide is the right direction for a tool that tells you whether to trust a number.

Training runs

This calculator cannot estimate training-run variation from aggregate before-and-after counts, and it does not pretend to. Two success totals contain no information about what would change if the same recipe were trained again, and on the evidence below that is usually the larger source of variation. Every verdict here is therefore a best case: the answer you would get if retraining changed nothing. It never gets better with more episodes.

The reference class

What it cannot do

It sees two numbers. It cannot know whether the two periods ran the same product mix, whether the scorer changed, whether the easy items moved to another line, or whether anyone picked the window after seeing the result. Every one of those breaks the comparison more thoroughly than the arithmetic here can detect.

Your data

Nothing is uploaded. The numbers live in this page and in the fragment after the # in the address bar, which browsers never send to a server. Sharing the link shares the inputs with whoever you send it to, so treat the link the way you would treat the numbers.

Provenance

This is the free version.

It checks the arithmetic on two numbers you already have. What it cannot do is tell you whether those numbers were measured on the right thing, in the right window, by a scorer whose own error rate anyone has checked. That is the paid one.
