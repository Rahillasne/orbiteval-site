Where it’s going

Apartments first. Then any building with an elevator.

    Trash bags collected from doorsteps on a separate run, in a sealed bin. Never on the same trip as food or parcels. Residents already pay $25–40 a month for this.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is this a real robot yet?

Not yet. The design and the parts list are real, and building the first prototype is our next step. Everything in our film and images is rendered from our CAD models, not filmed. When we have footage of the robot working, we’ll show it as it is.

What does the building have to change?

Very little: a spot against a wall for the station, one outlet, Wi-Fi or a cell signal, and a fob for the robot. Nothing is wired into the elevator.

What if the elevator is full?

R2 waits for the next car. It never asks riders to press buttons for it, and it doesn’t board during a fire alarm.

Can it get into apartments?

No. R2 never opens apartment doors and never gets apartment access. It hands parcels over at the door or leaves them on the mat.

What about doors without an automatic opener?

Where there’s a wheelchair push plate, it uses that. Where there isn’t, it asks someone nearby to hold the door and tells the front desk if no one comes. Buildings don’t have to add openers.

Is it safe around kids and pets?

It slows to walking pace near people, has a soft bumper all the way round and an emergency stop on top, and we’re designing it toward UL 3300, the safety standard for robots in public spaces.
