The problem

Eight identical models. Eight different scores.

These are eight ACT policies trained on byte-identical data — same dataset, same recipe, same 100k steps — differing only in training seed, and evaluated on the same 500 episodes at the same eval seed.

Your dashboard shows the average. You deploy the tasks.

A suite average shrinks noise by roughly √(number of tasks), so the headline number looks stable while every individual task keeps its full swing. We measured both quantities on the same retrains, in two policy classes.

Per-task retrain σ is 2.6–3.2× the suite σ measured beside it — 11.00 pp against 4.23 for π₀.₅, 6.69 against 2.11 for SmolVLA (both per-task readings on libero_object).
Damage is task-idiosyncratic — cross-task correlation 0.011, so regressions cancel in the mean instead of showing up in it.
Checkpoint sweeps buy about nothing. Across 36 promotion decisions in two classes, sweeps reported +1.7 to +2.3 pp and delivered ≈0 held-out.

The product

One engine. Three places it decides.

Orbit sits on top of the eval harness you already run. It does not replace your tracking — it turns what you already log into a decision you can defend.

A status check that can say no.

Wire the gate between your eval harness and your OTA tooling. Before a retrained checkpoint ships, it designs the cheapest decisive experiment, then rules — with quantified error rates, winner's-curse correction and per-task regression flags.

Exits 0/1/2/3/4 — SHIP, HOLD, INVALID, COLLECT-MORE, UNRESOLVABLE. Drops straight into CI.
Prices the retrain lottery, so lottery-sized swings are labelled instead of paging you at 2am.
Refuses rather than guesses: an eval it cannot legitimately price returns INVALID with the reason attached.

The paid product

Your cell, measured. Then every decision priced.

Because noise floors do not transfer, onboarding is a measurement: we run a retrain battery against your stack and hand you your own row in the atlas. From then on the gate prices every ship decision you make.

Week 1 — capture. One read-only command over your existing training output. LeRobot already writes per-episode eval logs by default; nothing to instrument.
Weeks 2–4 — your σ. A retrain battery on your data and your budget produces your cell, with confidence intervals and scope stamps.
Quarter 1 — shadow mode. The gate runs beside your decisions without changing them. At the end you get the divergence report: every call where it disagreed, with the evidence attached.
Then — the gate decides. In CI, on the ledger, with a certificate you can hand to a customer.

Why trust it

We publish the predictions we got wrong.

Every result behind this product was pre-registered — decision bands frozen and hashed before a single episode was scored. When a prediction failed, it stayed on the record and its stated consequence was refused. When a pre-check failed, the pre-committed demotion applied and the weaker claim shipped.

That discipline is not decoration. A product whose job is to tell you your win was noise cannot itself be in the business of favourable rounding.
